Data sources & method

You provide names — the tool checks authoritative, free public sources live, by itself, and records the list version it screened against.

Sanctions 90+ lists · live · timestamped

V2 no longer uses a fixed list set: it reads the public OpenSanctions data catalog live and screens against every source list in the Consolidated Sanctions collection — 90+ official lists — each pulled free (no key) as a daily-updated feed from the OpenSanctions data CDN. Coverage spans the UN Security Council; the EU and its member states; the UK (OFSI/FCDO); the US across OFAC, BIS, State, DHS and DoD; Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Switzerland, Japan; the Gulf & Middle East; Asia-Pacific, Africa, Latin America and more — plus multilateral debarment and wanted lists (e.g. World Bank, regional development banks) where published. New lists added by OpenSanctions are picked up automatically. Every match keeps its provenance — which list(s) and authority designated it — and each list's own publication date is captured as its as-of stamp and recorded on every screening reference. Lists that fail to download on a given run are reported, never silently dropped; a curated ~38-list set is the built-in fallback if the catalog is unreachable.

Fast server-side index new · rebuilt on every deploy

All OpenSanctions collections below are downloaded once on the server when the site is deployed and indexed by phonetic name key. Your browser no longer downloads ~100 lists: each name is checked in milliseconds via /api/screen (names are sent only to this site's own server, never to third parties, and are not stored). If the index is unreachable the app falls back to downloading the lists in the browser as before.

Wanted, crime, regulatory & debarment lists new · 150+ lists

From the OpenSanctions Crime, Regulatory and Debarment collections: Interpol red notices, FBI / Europol / NCA / national most-wanted lists, India NIA wanted and NSE debarred entities, terrorism lists, regulator enforcement actions (SEC, FINRA, FinCEN, Fed, OCC, MAS, DFSA, FINMA, AMF, ESMA…), disqualified directors (UK, Isle of Man, Guernsey) and multilateral / government debarment (World Bank, ADB, AfDB, IADB, EBRD, EU EDES, US SAM). A match is flagged Review: confirm identity before acting. US state Medicaid exclusion registers are left out as noise for KYC.

Quick checks: every result also links pre-filled searches of registers with no open bulk data — ICIJ Offshore Leaks, OpenCorporates, UK Companies House, India (SEBI, MCA, RBI, Enforcement Directorate, CIBIL wilful defaulters, FCRA) and Gulf / US / UK regulators.

Corporate ownership & control — UBO live · 2 sources

When a company is screened, the tool maps who owns and controls it from two free, open sources and screens every party found:

1. Natural-person owners & officers — Wikidata: owners / shareholders, parent organisation, founders, CEO/COO, chairperson, board members and directors. Each person is screened for sanctions, PEP status and adverse media.

2. Corporate ownership chain — GLEIF / LEI: the official Legal Entity Identifier record resolves the company’s direct parent, ultimate parent and subsidiaries (legal entities), each screened for sanctions and adverse media. GLEIF is free, open and keyless.

The company inherits the highest risk found among its principals, so a clean company with a sanctioned owner, a PEP director or a sanctioned parent is escalated and flagged. Coverage limit: Wikidata is incomplete for private firms and GLEIF carries corporate ownership only (not natural-person beneficial owners) — for a legally reliable UBO check, pair this with a corporate registry or a licensed KYB/UBO source.

Name matching fuzzy

Not exact string match. A layered matcher handles real-world name variation: Levenshtein edit-distance for typos/OCR noise, a transliteration fold for romanisation variants (Mohammed / Muhammad / Mohamad), and Soundex phonetic codes for sound-alikes (Rahman / Rehman). Scoring is token-based and order-independent. A score of ≥ 92% auto-confirms a match; 84–92% is flagged "Possible" for human review; below that is cleared. Every match shows its confidence score and the method used.

PEP & RCA 4 sources · live

PEP status is screened against four free sources, layered — any one can flag a name, and agreeing sources are shown as corroboration:

1. National PEP registers: 100+ country-level PEP lists — parliament & government registers, CIA World Leaders, RuPEP and more — are downloaded live from the free OpenSanctions PEP collection and indexed locally with the same fuzzy matcher used for sanctions.

2. Live Wikidata: checks whether the name holds or held political office, and shows the position. It also screens for RCAs (Relatives & Close Associates): it walks the family/associate graph two relationship steps deep (spouse, parent, child, sibling, grandparent, grandchild, aunt/uncle, niece/nephew, in-laws, business associates) and flags the person "PEP-linked" when a relation holds office. Under FATF guidance, RCAs warrant the same enhanced due diligence as the PEP.

3. Live Wikipedia: the person's profile description and lead paragraph are scanned for public-office language (minister, senator, ambassador, head of state…) — catching office-holders whose registers or Wikidata records are incomplete. The full article body (lead + early-life / personal-life / family sections) is also scanned for RCA ties stated in prose — e.g. "her grandfather, a former Chief Minister" — catching family links to PEPs that Wikidata's structured graph doesn't record. Prose-derived ties are always marked "per Wikipedia — verify".

4. Live Google News: when sources 1–3 are all clear, recent news coverage is scanned for political-office context as a catch-net. News alone is indicative, not an identity match — it requires 2+ corroborating headlines and is always flagged as "Possible PEP — verify". Coverage limit: open data is incomplete for many people and family ties, so a "no match" is not a guarantee — a licensed PEP database is needed for full RCA assurance.

Adverse / negative media live

Live Google News — recent headlines are scanned for adverse terms (allegations, arrests, fraud, lawsuits, sanctions, notices, orders, jail). Every name also gets a 1-click pre-filtered news search.
